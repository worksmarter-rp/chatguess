President Publishes Senator's Cell Number to Save Daylight, Reaches Voicemail

The most powerful man on Earth is being stonewalled by a full inbox.

By Staff Reporter, Bureau of Government Affairs
INVALID DATE  ·  GOVMENT.ORG

Govment.org photo illustration  |  INVALID DATE

WASHINGTON — In what administration officials are describing as an innovative deployment of executive soft power, President Trump on Saturday published the personal cellphone number of Sen. Tom Cotton (R-AR) on Truth Social, directing the citizens of Arkansas to contact their senator immediately regarding the nation's ongoing failure to eliminate the biannual resetting of clocks. The President's post, which reached an estimated 9 million followers, encouraged constituents to call Cotton specifically about golf, a sport whose enjoyment, the President noted, is materially diminished by darkness arriving one hour earlier than it otherwise might.

Reporters who dialed the number found the voicemail box full. The White House declined to comment on whether this outcome had been anticipated.

The Bureau of Presidential Communications confirmed that the post was not a hack, a draft accidentally published, or part of any known foreign influence operation. It was, a senior official explained on condition of anonymity, "the move." When asked whether there were additional legislative tools available to the office of the presidency, the official paused for a moment before thanking reporters for their time.

At issue is the Sunshine Protection Act, a bill that would make Daylight Saving Time permanent, sparing Americans the twice-yearly ritual of changing clocks and the three to five days of vague existential suffering that follow. The bill has been introduced in various forms since 2018. It has never passed. The Senate, which Cotton serves in with apparent comfort, has yet to bring it to a floor vote, a fact that the President characterized on Truth Social as "CRAZY," "RIDICULOUS," and, in a separate post, "not good."

Cotton, for his part, has expressed general support for permanent Daylight Saving Time, making him an unusual target for a crowdsourced phone-spam campaign. The Bureau of Senatorial Relations noted that calling a co-ideological ally's personal cell and filling his voicemail to capacity represents what scholars of legislative procedure might describe as "a nontraditional whipping strategy." Senator Cotton's office issued a statement saying the Senator "looks forward to continuing to work with the administration." The statement did not address the phone number.

The 119th Congress, which is currently concluding its session, has passed 63 bills into law — a figure that positions it as the least productive two-year legislative session in more than fifty years, according to data compiled by researchers who apparently spent time doing this. For context, the 80th Congress, famously denounced by President Truman in 1948 as the "Do-Nothing Congress," passed 906 bills. Officials stress that comparing raw bill counts is methodologically imperfect and that quality, not quantity, defines legislative success, before declining to list the bills.

The President has identified Daylight Saving Time as a priority alongside border security, energy production, and what multiple Truth Social posts have characterized as the fundamental wrongness of losing an hour of evening light. Horologists reached for comment noted that the sun's behavior is, in the strictest sense, unrelated to congressional scheduling. They were not quoted in any official communications.

As of press time, Senator Cotton's voicemail remained full. Somewhere in Arkansas, it is either an hour earlier or an hour later than it was last week, depending on the calendar, a detail that continues to affect approximately 331 million Americans and at least one active golf game.
