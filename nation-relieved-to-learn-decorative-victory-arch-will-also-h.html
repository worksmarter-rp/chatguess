Nation Relieved to Learn Decorative Victory Arch Will Also House Snipers

Administration upgrades proposed 250-foot gilded monument to 'top grade Military Complex/Triumphal Arch,' because apparently those are the same thing now.

By Staff Reporter, Bureau of Government Affairs
INVALID DATE  ·  GOVMENT.ORG

Govment.org photo illustration  |  INVALID DATE

WASHINGTON — The administration moved swiftly this week to reassure a nervous public that the proposed 250-foot gilded triumphal arch slated for construction somewhere in the vicinity of the National Mall will not merely be decorative, announcing that the monument will also function as a fully operational military installation complete with rooftop snipers, stored ammunition, drone launching capabilities, and, presumably, a gift shop, though the gift shop has not been confirmed.

President Trump, citing an urgent request from the U.S. military, announced the upgrade in characteristically precise architectural terms, describing the revised project as a "top grade Military Complex/Triumphal Arch" — a building typology that does not currently exist in any known engineering handbook, zoning code, or 5,000 years of recorded construction history. The Bureau of Monumental Dual-Use Infrastructure confirmed that the slash between "Military Complex" and "Triumphal Arch" is doing significant structural work in the announcement.

"The military specifically asked for this," said an unnamed official familiar with the project, adding that the military's request was described internally as "totally unprompted." The official declined to name which branch of the military had determined that what it urgently required was a gilded arch modeled on Napoleonic Paris, but emphasized that the request was very urgent and very military.

The Arc de Triomphe in Paris, upon which the American project is loosely modeled, was completed in 1836 after thirty years of construction, stands 164 feet tall, and has never housed a sniper. Historians note this represents the most significant proposed departure from the source material since someone decided the Eiffel Tower did not need a moat. The French government has not commented, as the French government has more pressing concerns and also finds this exhausting.

A federal court is currently weighing whether the arch project is legal under existing statutes governing construction on or near the National Mall, a procedural matter the president did not address in his announcement. Legal observers note that this places the administration in the novel position of seeking, implicitly, a construction permit for a military installation whose foundational monument has not yet been ruled permissible to build. The Bureau of Sequential Logic declined to comment by press time, citing ongoing reorganization.

Architects reached for comment noted that integrating sniper positions into a triumphal arch is not, technically, without precedent, as medieval fortified gates performed a broadly similar function. They were careful to note, however, that medieval fortified gates were not typically gilded, were not typically proposed for the capital of a constitutional republic during an active judicial review, and were not typically announced via social media post that also mentioned the Arc de Triomphe being "frankly, a little small."

Residents of the Washington metropolitan area expressed measured relief at the news. "I was worried it was going to be purely ceremonial," said one unnamed constituent, who added that she had not previously been worried about this. A separate unnamed official confirmed that the drone storage component would not affect the arch's "elegant sight lines," and that the ammunition would be stored "tastefully."

The project remains in the planning phase, pending resolution of the lawsuit, finalization of a design, identification of a construction site, completion of an environmental review, and a determination of what, precisely, a triumphal arch commemorates when it is built before any triumph has occurred. These details are expected to be addressed at a future date, in a future announcement, by a future bureau.
